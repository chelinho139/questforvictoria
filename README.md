# Quest For Victoria

A co-op campaign videogame. Web build on Phaser 3 + TypeScript.

## Run

```bash
npm install
npm run dev      # esbuild watch + server (game page and online play) on http://localhost:3000
npm run build    # one-off bundles: public/dist/game.js (the browser) and dist/server/ (online play)
npm test         # the automated tests (see Tests)
npm run verify   # type-check + tests + build
npm run bot      # a test player for online play (see Online co-op)
npm run i18n     # strings in the source still missing a Spanish translation (see Languages)
```

## Layout

- `src/sim/` — engine-free game rules. `Game.ts` is one campaign (the story, quests, the day, its regions and heroes); `Region.ts` is one place (its `RegionMap`, creatures, drops, trees, rocks, scenes); `Hero.ts` is one player. `Sim.ts` is the single-player facade the Phaser layer reads.
- `src/net/` — online play shared by browser and server: the messages (`protocol.ts`), the whitelist of commands a browser may send (`commands.ts`), the browser's socket (`Connection.ts`) and `NetSim`, the Sim a browser plays online.
- `src/server/` — the game server, bundled to `dist/server/` and started by `server.js`: rooms, the 20-a-second loop, and a `Session` per player that builds their snapshots.
- `src/data/` — tunables and pixel-art sources: skills, enemies, icon/sprite maps.
- `src/phaser/` — Phaser layer: `BootScene` is the loading screen (logo, progress bar) and builds every texture in steps, `GameScene` ticks the sim and renders the world, `PcHudScene` is the Stardew/Terraria-style PC HUD (parchment unit frames, wooden action bar, day dial with gold box), `MobileHudScene` is the one-thumb touch HUD kept for a mobile build. `PLATFORM` in `src/phaser/config.ts` picks one. The canvas fills the window at an integer pixel scale. `audio/` makes and plays the sounds (see Sound).
- `src/i18n/` — languages: the game is written in English and the browser shows it in English or Spanish (see Languages).
- `public/` — static site; `public/dist/` is generated.
- `index.html` — the original single-file prototype the game is seeded from.

## Controls (PC)

Action bar (bottom): twelve slots on `1`–`6`, `Q`, `E`, `R`, `F`, `G`, `M`; each key casts whatever you put in its slot (drag spells there from the spellbook, `P`). Clicking a slot casts it too; hover over it for what the spell does in full (its mana and cooldown, the spellbook's description, and in red why it can't be cast right now). Keys: `src/data/actionBar.ts`; spells: `src/data/spells.ts`.

Rev (one-button rotation) is an advanced option for higher levels: off by default and not on the bar. Turn it on in the ⚙ panel under **Advanced** ("Rev on the action bar"); then `C` fires the next step of the sequence (turns Rev on), `Z` toggles Rev, `X` toggles Rev auto, and the next step is outlined in orange on the bar.

Mouse: click (left or right) on the ground to walk there, hold to keep steering toward the cursor. Click an enemy to lock it. Keyboard movement cancels a click-to-move. Routes go around water, rocks and walls (A* over the tile grid in `src/sim/pathfind.ts`, string-pulled into straight legs).

Other: WASD/arrows move, Space jump, Tab cycle target.

## Dev settings

Press `O` or `` ` `` (backtick), or click the cog on the menu bar. The panel changes things live and remembers them across reloads (localStorage key `qfv-dev-settings`):

- **Documents**: *The Lore* and *Prologue & Act I* (the build spec) open in the book viewer in a new tab, so the game keeps running; its *Back to the game* closes the tab. New specs go in `DOC_LINKS` (`src/phaser/dev/DevMenu.ts`) and `SPECS` (`server.js`).
- **Time**: clock slider, Dawn / Noon / Sunset / Midnight, day speed (pause, 1× = 10-minute day, up to 600×).
- **Weather**: what it is doing now; Auto (the schedule) or hold it Clear, Rain or Storm; *Strike lightning*. Held weather isn't remembered across reloads.
- **Graphics**: art style (the six candidates, switched instantly), HD hero, day/night lighting on/off, darkness strength, clouds on/off.
- **Sound**: volume (all the way down is off), and a sound board: pick any sound to hear it, in groups: each class's spells and what follows them (an arrow landing, the trap springing, the horse coming), the interface, the world, the creatures, and the voices.
- **Player**: god mode, infinite mana, no cooldowns, move speed, full heal, back to start.
- **Difficulty**: Normal, Hard or Nightmare for this campaign, at once (the creatures already out take their new health); it goes into the save. Online it is the whole room's.
- **Enemies**: freeze, kill all, respawn all (back to the starting creatures), reset game, spawn any creature near you.
- **Debug**: draw the click-to-move path, FPS counter, skip loading screen.

Shortcuts outside the panel: `I` opens the inventory, `T` cycles the time of day, `L` toggles lighting, `V` cycles the art style and `H` the HD hero (Shift goes back).

The top-right dial shows the time of day: sun and moon arc over the hills, the sky shifts from blue to sunset to a starry night, and the label names the part of day and the day number (`src/phaser/render/DayDial.ts`).

## Art

All art is generated in code at load time; there are no image files.

- **The game is drawn in HD · Silhouette colours.** All new art is made only for it. The other styles are kept as backups: Settings › Graphics › *Backup art styles* shows their buttons and turns on the `V` (style) and `H` (hero) keys; switching it off goes back to HD · Silhouette.
- **Art styles** (stored in localStorage `qfv-art-style` while the backups are on):
  - **HD · Silhouette colours** (the game's art): the HD sprites (`hdSprites.ts`, `hdHero.ts`, `hdHeroes.ts`) recoloured into the Silhouette palette by `regrade.ts`. `H` picks its hero: three knights (K1 Knight, K2 Squire, K3 Silver knight, each with an idle animation and a 6-frame walk) or five heroes modelled on the approved board looks.
  - **S1h**, **C2 · Ink contrast**, **C3 · Rich summer** and **1 · Silhouette**: no-outline 4× pixel art in `src/phaser/render/styles/gameArt.ts` (one set of shapes, three palettes; S1h swaps in the big-head hooded hero).
- Each style returns the same parts (`styleArt.ts`); `textures.ts` redraws the textures in place on a switch, and `art.ts` tracks each texture's display scale and frame count.
- **Icons**: the 14 action icons are hand-designed 20×20 items in `hdIcons.ts`, shared by every style.
- **UI kit** in `src/phaser/render/uiKit.ts`: 9-slice parchment and wood panels, inset slots, selection ring, bar insets, heart/mana/coin/skull/star icons.
- **Logo and loading screen** in `src/phaser/render/splash.ts`.

## Sound

Like the art, every sound is made in code at load time; there are no sound files.

- **What sounds**: every spell when it's cast (a spell's key is its sound's name), and what follows: an arrow landing (each special arrow has its own), a sorceress's bolt landing (fire, frost, a spell broken, a pillar of flame, each leap of lightning, a Pyroblast's explosion), a bear trap springing, the horse coming and being left. Everyone nearby hears these.
- **The world** (`WorldSound`, heard nearby): the auto-attacks (a swing; a shot and its arrow landing; a bolt from the staff and its pop), a blow landing on a hero, each chop and the tree falling, each swing of the pick and the rock breaking, the shaman's fireball leaving and landing, and the Bell-Ringer's bell, which swells out of silence and stops dead (as the story tells it), once for each ring.
- **The creatures** (`sounds` in each `EnemyDef`, `src/data/enemies.ts`; required, so a new creature has to have them): noticing you, attacking (the swing or bite; the blow landing is the hero's sound), hurt (not by a bleed), dying (or crumbling at dawn), and as it fits: wandering (a slime's blorp about every 18 s, a cow's moo about once a minute (`idleEvery`) and never two moos within 6 s, decided with `Math.random` so the game's dice stay out of it), climbing out of the ground, starting a spell. Goblins cackle, the shaman chants, ogres roar, skeletons chatter and come apart bone by bone, bone hounds bark, the Bell-Ringer groans; the test boss borrows the ogre's.
- **Voices** (`VoiceSound`): everyone who talks (each NPC, and the Bell-Ringer in his scenes) speaks in a babble of their own, made from a `Speaker` (pitch, mouth size, pace, lilt, roughness, a tremble, an echo): Aldric gravelly and slow, Nan high and trembling, Maud soft and trailing off, Pike clipped and loud, Odo sing-song in his chapel, Tobin quick and chatty, Bram deep. Each voice has four takes, picked at random. The dialog window speaks each new line, a scene each line by a speaker with a voice, and a new line cuts off the last (`Sfx.say`).
- **The interface** (heard only by the hero it happens to): a level (a harp and a chord), a talent, a quest taken, its goals done and handed in (a fanfare), a journal entry, gold (a kill, buying, selling), an item picked up, gear on or off, eating, cooking (food), smelting (bars), smithing (the rest at the forge), woodwork (bows), building, a perfect-timed tap, a mistake (a spell you can't cast, gold you don't have; it can't repeat faster than every 0.4 s), dying and coming back, and a window opening or closing (`GameScene` watches the windows, so a key, a button, Esc or another window taking its place all count). The jingles are tuned to D major so they sit together, and play at their own pitch.
- **Recipes** (`src/phaser/audio/sounds.ts`): one per sound, built from shared pieces (a whoosh, a thump, a crunch, a bowstring, struck metal, a voice through mouth shapes, crackle, chimes, a flame, ice, a lightning zap, a shimmer), so the warrior's blows, the archer's arrows and the sorceress's fire, frost and lightning each sound like one family. `loud` sets a sound's loudness against the others (1: the biggest finishing blows); the renderer brings it there and rounds off any peak.
- **The synthesizer** (`src/phaser/audio/synth.ts`): oscillators, filtered noise, a plucked string, struck metal, saturation and a small reverb, rendered sample by sample into a buffer. It uses no Web Audio and no Phaser, so the tests render every sound in Node.
- **Playing** (`src/phaser/audio/Sfx.ts`): the loading screen renders every sound, a few per step (`soundJobs`; about 0.4 s for all of them). A recipe's `takes` renders it several times, and each play picks one; its `gap` keeps it from repeating too fast. The sim sends `sound` as a region event (what, and where) or as a hero event (what, for that hero alone: `Hero.hear`), and `Sfx` plays it through Phaser's Web Audio context. A sound with a place is at full volume within 160 screen px of your hero, fades out by 720, is panned left or right, and is up to 4% higher or lower each time. Online the server's hero makes the sound and it travels in the snapshot, like the effects.
- **The weather** (`WeatherSound`, `src/phaser/audio/ambience.ts`, `WeatherAudio.ts`): rain, a downpour and storm wind are seamless loops (rendered with the rest at load, their ends crossfaded into their starts), faded in and out with the weather; thunder comes in three distances, close (a crack, then the roll), out of sight and far off, two takes each, delayed after its flash and panned toward it. Indoors all of it plays through a low-pass, as heard through a roof.
- **Adding a sound**: give it an id in `SoundId` (`src/sim/types.ts`: a `FollowSound`, `WorldSound` or `UiSound`; a creature's is a `CreatureSound` in `src/data/enemies.ts`) and a recipe in `SOUNDS`, and emit it with `region.sound(id, x, y)` or `hero.hear(id)` (a creature's are emitted for it). A new spell needs only a recipe under its key, and a new NPC only a `Speaker`; the type-check won't pass without them, nor without a sound board name for a new `UiSound` or `WorldSound`.

## HUD

- **Action bar** (bottom centre): twelve slots you fill yourself. A new hero knows only Thrust and Slash; the rest unlock with levels (Rend 2, Charge 3, Mount 4, War Cry 5, Interrupt 6, Execute 7, Whirlwind 9, Mortal Strike 11) and land on the bar by themselves. Execute (and the archer's Kill Shot and the sorceress's Incinerate) can be used once every 20 s. Every hero also knows **Revive** (see below). The Rev slot sits left of it when Rev is turned on in Settings.
- **Mana** (`mp`, `mpLevel` in `src/data/classes.ts`; `MANA_COMBAT`, `MANA_REST` in `src/sim/Hero.ts`): each class has its own pool, small at first and growing every level to make room for the spells it brings: the warrior 40 and 8 more a level (232 at 25), the archer 45 and 10 (285), the sorceress 50 and 10 (290), whose spells also cost the most (her spellbook is her weapon; the archer's cost a little more than the warrior's). It comes back at only 0.5 a second in a fight (for 3 s after you cast, strike or are struck; over within a second once nothing is after you) and 5 a second out of one, so at level 1 a fight affords about four spells and the rest is your weapon; you wake at camp with half of it. `npx tsx tests/mana-report.ts` prints how low each build's mana runs in the balance fights.
- **Falling and Revive** (`Hero.down`, `Hero.updateRevive`): a hero who falls while anyone else in the region is still up lies where they fell ("Wait for a friend to revive you"). A friend kneels by them and casts **Revive** (on everyone's bar, slot `G`): 8 s standing still within reach, broken by a step or a blow; they get up with 40% of their health. Once nobody in the region is left standing, everyone down wakes back at camp (the safe fire, or the road), as a hero alone always does. Online the fallen state travels in the snapshot (`down`).
- **Spellbook** (`P`, the red tome): every spell with what it does, its cost and cooldown (as your talents have them), and how you get it ("Unlocks at level 9", "Learn it from the Blade talent Sunder"). Drag a learned spell onto the bar; while the book is open, drag spells along the bar to move them or off it to remove them.
- **Menu bar** (bottom right): Inventory `I`, Spellbook `P`, Talents `N`, Crafting `K`, Quests `J`, Settings `O`, Controls `/`. A button lights up while its window is open; hover for a tooltip. Windows on the left (crafting, quests, controls) replace each other; the inventory has the right side; `Esc` closes.
- **Controls** (`/`, the book): every key in one place.

## Levels and talents

- **Experience** (`src/data/talents.ts`): you start at level 1 (max 25); a level takes 100 XP at first and 25% more each level after. Kills give XP (slime 10, bone hound 16, skeleton 24, the Bell-Ringer 150, Sir Garrick 250…), quests give 20 to 200, and felling trees, breaking rocks, crafting and building give a little. The Prologue's quests pay 160 XP in all, Act I's 460 and Act II's 1,630, which with the fighting brings a hero to about level 3–4 by Millbrook, 6–7 by the end of Act I and about 11 by the Weeping Bridge (to tune in play). A slain creature is back at its post after 60–90 s (`RESPAWN` in `src/data/enemies.ts`), and carries only a few coins (`gold`, a min–max roll; beasts none): gold comes mostly from quests. A creature dragged more than `LEASH` (600) from its post, or left more than `STRAY` (240) from it when its foe dies or hides, gives up and walks home untouchable (*EVADE*), healing on the way (`src/sim/Region.ts`). Each level gives a talent point, +5 maximum health and a full heal. A level is the biggest moment on screen: a fanfare (timpani, brass, harp and bells), a gold flash and a sunburst behind **LEVEL UP!**, a column of gold light on the hero for everyone there, and the level on a gold badge at the corner of your portrait glows for a while after. The purple bar above the action bar shows progress (hover for the numbers).
- **Talents** (`N`, or the star on the menu bar, which glows while points are waiting): three trees side by side, Diablo 2 / WoW style. A tier opens once you have 4 more points in that tree (the capstone needs 16); arrows mean a talent needs another one maxed first, and a talent that improves a spell waits until you have learned it (Tracker, for Hunter's Mark, opens at level 5). Click to learn a rank, hover for what this rank and the next do; Reset gives every point back (free for now).
  - Each tree teaches two **abilities** you can't get any other way (marked with a purple diamond): one in tier 3 and the capstone. They go into your spellbook and onto your bar.
  - **Blade**: Honed Edge (+damage), Precise Thrust, Lingering Rend, Keen Eye (critical hits), Twin Cuts (Slash combo), Bloodletting, Brutal Strikes (crit damage), Improved Mortal Strike, Warden's Edge (crits make the target bleed), Executioner (Execute below 35%), Reaping Blows (mana on kill). Abilities: **Sunder** (the target takes 20% more damage from you for 10 s) and capstone **Deathblow** (30 damage, ready again if it kills).
  - **Fury**: Battle Rage (auto-attack damage), Booming Voice and Commanding Shout (War Cry), Momentum and Stunning Charge, Flurry (faster swings), Bloodthirst (heal on hit), Improved Whirlwind and Cyclone, Enrage (below half health), Rampage (kills fire up War Cry). Abilities: **Bloodrage** (15% health for 40 mana) and capstone **Berserk** (10 s of 50% faster swings and +20% damage).
  - **Warden**: Toughness (armor), Vitality (health), Woodsman and Prospector (gathering), Shield Wall, Second Wind (health per kill), Camp Cook (food), Steadfast (regeneration), Last Warden (survive a killing blow once a minute), Watchful (Interrupt), Swift Feet (walk, ride and mount faster). Abilities: **Shield Bash** (needs a shield: 6 damage, 2 s stun, stops spells) and capstone **Last Stand** (heal 30%, take 30% less damage for 8 s).
- The ⚙ Settings panel's Player section has **+1 level**, **+100 XP** and **Reset talents** for testing.

## People, lore and quests

- **People** (`src/data/npcs.ts`; art in `tools/heroes/npcart.py` for Aldric and `tools/heroes/villagers.py` for the rest): **Warden Aldric** at his camp by Lake Ellory, and in Millbrook **Nan Merrow** (the queen's old nurse), **Maud Ashdown** (the miller's widow), **Sergeant Pike** (the Steward's herald), **Father Odo** (the priest), **Tobin** (a peddler) and **Bram** (the smith). Act II adds **Wren Ashdown** (Marcian's sister), the charcoal-burners of Kilnholt (**Hesketh Coll** the kiln-master, **Ada Coll**, **Grandad Pell**, **Mother Dunn**), **Bess Tanner** (a widow following the lights), and **Sir Garrick**'s ghost on the bridge (art in `tools/heroes/villagers2.py`). Click someone to talk (you walk over first): an introduction the first time, then greetings that change with the story and topics to ask about. People come and go with the story and the night (`Region.syncNpcs`: someone listed twice stands at the first place whose condition holds: Wren on the mill step, at Aldric's camp, at the oak, by the river at the thorn wall). The game's story lives in `docs/lore.md` (see *Lore* below), and the plans in `docs/act1.md` and `docs/act2.md` (http://localhost:3000/act1.html, /act2.html).
- **Trading**: Tobin and Bram are traders (`shop` in their NpcDef). Choose *"Let me see what you have."* to open the trade window (docked left): what they sell at each item's `price`, and everything in your bag they would buy, at a twentieth of its price (`SELL_CUT` in `items.ts`; anything worth less than a coin to them they won't take; shift-click sells a whole stack). Unique quest gear has no price and can't be sold. A trader's stock can grow with the story (`shop.more`: more to sell once a condition holds; `Game.shopStock`).
- **The grey postman** (`wanderers` in a region): a ghost who walks his round of Millbrook's lanes at night with a faint cold light, fading as you come near. You can't fight or talk to him yet; clicking him says why. **The grey lantern** in the Weepwood is a wanderer too, with `out`: let anyone come close and it goes out for good (a flag, a scene), leaving grey dust and a dead moth.
- **Crows** (`crows` in a region): perched about since the bells, turning their heads; one flies off when you come near (drawn only, `WorldRenderer.drawCrows`).
- **Companions** (`src/data/companions.ts`, `src/sim/Companion.ts`): someone from the story who walks with the heroes for a while: Wren, in the Weepwood (Act II, `docs/act2.md`). While the story has her along (`when`) and a hero is in one of her `regions`, she follows the nearest hero (on a path round things, at their side again if left far behind), shoots whatever is fighting the heroes or what the hero she follows is fighting (her kills, gold and experience are that hero's), and grows with the heroes' level. Creatures can go for her; badly hurt she drops to one knee (`down` seconds) instead of dying, and creatures leave her be till she's up. Snares and webs hold her too. When the heroes leave her regions she goes home (`home`) and waits there until one of them comes back for her. She calls out what she sees (`barks`: going down and getting up, something dropping out of the trees, a kind of creature or object coming in sight, nightfall), shown over her head and in the log (`Region.say`). The Game decides which region she is in (`Game.placeCompanions`); online she travels in the snapshots (`cp`). She is someone you can talk to as well (`npc`: her dialog and quests, her ! and ? over her head), and is drawn from her own frames (`art`: walk, idle, the shot, down on one knee; `WREN_ART` in `tools/heroes/villagers2.py`).
- **Quests** (`src/data/quests.ts`) come from NPCs: a gold **!** over someone's head means a new quest, a gold **?** means one is ready to hand in, a grey **…** means you are on it. Accept in the dialog (goals and rewards are shown), do it, and hand it in (to the giver, or to `turnIn`) for gold, gear and documents. Goals count only after you accept: kill (an enemy kind), build, craft, reach (a place, in a region), talk (to someone), collect (items in your bag, optionally handed over), interact (use an object) or flag (something has happened). ## World, story and saving

- **Regions** (`src/data/regions/`): the world is made of regions joined by exits. Each region is a hand-authored text layout (one character per tile, see `TILE_CHARS` in `src/sim/map.ts`), a corruption ring (0 untouched to 5 Thornhallow), named spots (`start` and arrival points), exits (walk into an area to travel, fading through black; an exit can be closed with a story condition and a message), creature spawns, NPCs and objects, each optionally behind a story condition, and scenes to play on arrival. The map edge is solid except on exit tiles. What you build or drop in a region is kept when you leave; trees, rocks and creatures reset. The Greenmarch is the start; *Test Field* is a dev-only region (Settings › Regions) with a page, a board, a chest, an arrival scene and a test boss.
- **Ground rendering** (`buildRegionGround` in `textures.ts`): each region's ground is baked on arrival and sliced into 2048-px chunks; the whole world lives in one Phaser layer that is destroyed and rebuilt when you change region. The iso projection offset is fixed (`ISO_OX`), so maps up to 200 rows deep need no other changes.
- **The Blackthorn** (`ringDarkness` in `src/sim/daylight.ts`): the deeper a region's ring, the darker and greyer its light; from ring 2 the night comes earlier and goes later (`ringHour`, `Region.isNight`); ring 4 never gets brighter than dusk, ring 5 is always night, and from ring 3 the dead walk by day. A region under the grave-mist (`dusk`) is dusk all day, and its dead walk by day too.
- **Story flags and conditions** (`src/data/story.ts`, `src/sim/story.ts`): `Sim.flags` records what has happened; a `Cond` (flag, not, quest status, level, all, any) gates NPCs, spawns, exits, objects, topics, quests and scenes; an `Effect` sets or clears flags, adds documents, gives items or plays a scene.
- **NPCs** (`src/data/npcs.ts`): an introduction the first time, greetings that change with the story (`greetings`), topics to ask about (`topics`, each with a condition, `once`, and an effect), and `onMeet` effects. Where each stands is up to the region.
- **Objects** (region `objects`): a page in the grass (it glints), a notice board, a chest. Click one, or press the gather key next to it: it says its line, applies its effect, and `once` objects are gone for good. Art in `tools/heroes/objects.py` → `objectArt.ts`.
- **Journal** (`src/data/docs.ts`): diary pages, letters, proclamations, notes and lace favours you find go into the Journal tab of the quest log (`J`), grouped by kind, unread ones marked; click one to read it.
- **Scenes** (`src/data/scenes.ts`): scripted beats played while you watch: lines (click, Space or Enter for the next), waits, fades, the camera looking at a tile, banners and effects. Input and fighting wait while a scene plays; each scene plays once per game.
- **Bosses** (`EnemyDef.boss`): a big health bar at the top while you fight one, phases as its health falls (a `bossPhase` event for its behaviour), and a story flag when it dies; give its spawn `when: { not: flag }` so it stays dead.
- **Characters and saving** (`src/sim/save.ts`, `src/phaser/saveStore.ts`): single player has characters like multi player, up to 8, each with their own campaign save in the browser (the list in localStorage `qfv-chars`, each save in `qfv-save:<id>`). A save is written shortly after anything important (a quest, a level, a region, gear, a flag), every 30 seconds, and when the tab is hidden. The old single save (`qfv-save`) becomes the first character the first time the game opens, with its name and hero. With the loading screen skipped, the game carries on as the character played last.
- **Difficulty** (`src/data/difficulty.ts`): **Normal** (the game as balanced: creatures hit 1.3× as hard as their own numbers and have 1.15× the health), **Hard** (1.95× and 1.4×: half again as hard as Normal) or **Nightmare** (2.6× and 1.75×: twice Normal's blows). Chosen on the new-character screen in single player, and when opening a room online (it starts as hard as the host's campaign was); kept in the campaign's save (saves from before have none: Normal), and starting over keeps it. Every blow a creature lands goes through `Region.strike` (hits, bolts, leaps, fireballs, the bell's toll, on heroes and companions alike); creatures' health is set on spawn (`Region.maxHp`; cows and deer stay as they are), and a change mid-game rescales the creatures already out (`Game.setDifficulty`). Settings › Difficulty changes it. **Online, the creatures scale with the heroes in their region** (`partyScale`, `PARTY_HP`, `PARTY_DMG` in `src/data/difficulty.ts`, counted every tick by `Region.updateParty`): for each hero beyond the first, +60% health and +10% damage (two heroes: ×1.6 and ×1.1; four: ×2.8 and ×1.3), so a pack takes a party about as long as it takes one hero (two heroes clear it in about 0.8 of the time, four in 0.7, as the blows are shared too). Someone arriving or leaving rescales the creatures already out, as hurt as they were, and everyone there is told.
- **The start** (`src/phaser/ui/`): `MainMenu` (Single Player | Multi Player, under the logo; Single Player shows the character played last). Both modes then use the same two screens: `CharacterList` ("Your characters": a card per character in their own look and gear, with level, place and when last played; New character, Delete (asks once more), Play, Back; arrow keys, Enter, Esc) and `NewCharacter` (a name, a class and one of the HD heroes, with the animated preview; in single player also the difficulty). `SinglePlayer` keeps its characters in the browser, and Play starts the game; `Lobby` keeps them on the server, and Play goes on to the rooms.
- **Specs**: the plan for each slice of the campaign is a Markdown file in `docs/` read in the same book viewer, e.g. http://localhost:3000/act1.html for `docs/act1.md` (Prologue and Act I, built) and http://localhost:3000/act2.html for `docs/act2.md` (Act II, built).
- **The regions so far**: **the Greenmarch** (72×60, ring 0: Lake Ellory, the shore where you wake, Aldric's camp and tent, the hollow oak and Marcian's willow, two barrows, the goblins' camp; the road north to Millbrook; Ashford closed by the Steward's barricade and the Greyfang Hills by a rockfall), **Millbrook** (64×56, ring 1: the square with the well, Tobin's stall and Bram's smithy, the mill on the Lisle with its turning wheel, the bridge, Nan's cottage, the chapel and churchyard, blighted grass, wilted fields; the north road closed by black thorns), and **the Bell Tower** (indoors: the ground floor, the stair, the belfry). Act II's wood (ring 2): **the Weepwood** (80×64: Wren's camp, the spider dell, the Keening Hollow, Kilnholt inside its ring of kilns; the river path to Millbrook, the old road north behind a fallen black willow), **the King's Chase** (72×60: the King's Ride, the roofless hunting lodge and kennels, the Gallows Willow, the deer glades, Bonespine's rocks, the goblin outriders and their wall), **Heron Reach** (76×64: Heron Lodge, the ford and the Tower Guard, the Lisle Barrow, three Warden caches, the grave-mist across the road), **Heron Lodge** and **the Lisle Barrow** (indoors), and **the Weeping Bridge** (60×54, dusk all day: the old road through the bone spikes, the mourners' stones, the bridge and its two great willows). The Test Field and the Art Yard (every building and prop, for checking art) are dev-only.
- **Maps** are composed by `tools/world/maps.py` (lakes, rivers and roads as strokes, forest edges, fields, scattered trees and rocks, buildings and marks), which writes each region's `layout` and `props`: `python3 maps.py write`, `python3 maps.py preview millbrook out.png`. `python3 check.py greenmarch millbrook belltower weepwood kingschase heronreach heronlodge lislebarrow weepingbridge` checks that every person, creature, object and spot stands on ground you can walk on (so does `tests/act2.test.ts` for Act II). A prop can carry a story condition (`when`: the fallen willow until it is sawn, the mist until the last mark is read); when it stops holding the prop goes, live (`Region.syncProps`, online in the snapshots).
- **Buildings and big props** (`props` in a region, `src/data/props.ts`): cottages, Nan's cottage, the mill (its wheel animates), the chapel and bell tower, the smithy, the stall, the well, fences, Aldric's tent, barrows, the barricade, the rockfall, graves, the thorn wall, the bell, the interior walls, and the story trees; and Act II's (`tools/world/buildings2.py`): Wren's lean-to and drying rack, Kilnholt's turf huts, sawpit and long table, the thorn tunnel, the fallen willow, bone spikes, thickets, webbed willows, unmarked graves, standing and boundary stones, the hunting lodge and kennels, the Gallows Willow, goblin tents, wolf pens and wall, Heron Lodge, the Tower Guard's outpost, the Lisle Barrow, the grave-mist, the bridge's parapets, the great willows, Garrick's watch-fire and lean-to, the black vines, and inside: bunks, niches and the Thane's stone seat. Each has a footprint you can't walk through (or a mask: the chapel's door, a tree's trunk), sorts correctly with what walks in front of it or behind it (a faint silhouette shows you behind a building), and windows that light up at night with a warm pool of light. They are drawn by `tools/world/iso.py` (a small ray-caster for boxes, gable, lean-to and pyramid roofs, cylinders, boulders and mounds, with hand-set materials and pixel decals for doors and windows) and `tools/world/buildings.py`, straight in the Silhouette palette; `python3 gen_props.py` writes `src/phaser/render/propArt.ts`. The two story trees are painted like the forest's oaks (`src/phaser/render/storyTrees.ts`).
- **Ground** for the campaign (`src/phaser/render/groundArt.ts`): blighted grass, tilled fields, wilted fields, cobbles, the bridge deck and flagstones, in the layout as `,` `f` `w` `c` `=` `+`; `&` is solid dark rock round an interior. The Weepwood adds grey-green moss and leaf litter (`m`), the old road's cobbles with thorns through them (`r`) and fords of stepping stones (`F`); from ring 2 the rivers run dark.
- **Kinds of tree** (`src/data/trees.ts`, in the layout as `T` oak, `Y` black willow, `y` half-green willow, `K` black oak; painted in `src/phaser/render/woodTrees.ts` with stumps of their own): a black willow takes six chops and gives **black willow** (hard as horn) instead of logs; a black oak takes five.
- **Indoors** (`indoor` in a region) it is as dim as dusk all day, lit by the region's `lights` (torches). Walls facing a room stand tall behind it; walls in front of a room are cut low so you can see in.
- **As you play**: objects can come and go with the story and the night (a letter on a doorstep only after dark), can need a condition before they can be used (`need`, `needSay`) or take something from the bag (`cost`: three logs to light a hearth), scenes can play when a condition comes true while you are in a region (the bell at nightfall) or when you walk into an area (`triggers`), and conditions can test `{ night: true }`.
- **A region's own fires** (`structures` in a region): a kiln, a camp's fire once it is lit. They come and go with their condition, burn for good, are never saved, and are lit again each dawn if something put them out.
- **Holding a place through the night** (`hold` in a region, Kilnholt's ring of kilns): from dusk, while its condition holds, the dead keep coming out of the dark for the fires (one every `every` seconds, never more than `max` at once). At dawn, with at least `need` fires still burning, its flag is set; otherwise it says so and the fires are lit again for another night.
- **Waking by a safe fire** (`wake` in a region): a hero who falls there wakes at the first spot whose condition holds (a camp once its fire is lit; a lodge in another region), saying so; otherwise back on the road at `start`.

## Inventory, items, gathering and crafting

- **Inventory** (`I`, or the backpack on the menu bar; `Esc` closes): a Diablo 2 style panel docked on the right, hidden until opened. It shows the hero with their gear stats, a paper doll with 7 equipment slots (head, body, legs, feet, weapon, off-hand, trinket) and the 24-slot bag. Hover anything for its tooltip (gear in the bag compares with what you wear). Click gear in the bag to equip it, click worn gear to take it off, click food to eat it. Code: `src/phaser/ui/InventoryWindow.ts`.
- **Gear** (`src/data/items.ts`): adds attack (damage per hit), armor (each point takes a little off every hit), health, speed and chopping speed. A new hero has only a rusty sword and cloth trousers; everything better is earned, in tiers:
  - **Tier 1, leather and wood** (cap, tunic, trousers, boots, wooden shield, woodcutter's axe, pickaxe): slimes (about one item per three kills) and goblins.
  - **Tier 2, iron** (sword, kite shield, helm, chainmail, greaves) plus the amulet and swift boots: skeletons at night, ogres and shamans, and Bram's smithy.
  - **Unique** quest gear (`unique`, drawn like the common piece it is based on with `looks`): Edric's helm, Queen Elowen's amulet, the warm cloak (Maud), the sexton's lantern (a trinket that lights the dark around you). Act I adds bone hound fangs, black thorns, bread and bandages, and hound-leather trousers.
  - **Tier 3, steel and the Wardens' gear** (Act II): the steel sword, kite shield and helm (Bram, once he has Kilnholt's charcoal, or the forge with steel bars), the silk-strung recurve, the thornback jerkin, the fang necklace; and from quests the Warden's cloak, hood, boots, blade or longbow, the broodsilk leggings, Pell's hunting horn (creatures you hit stay angry at you longer: `anger`), Bonespine's mantle, the Thane's torc and the royal guard greaves (the first plate). Act II's materials and food: thornback pelts, spider silk, black willow, charcoal, steel bars, venison (roast it at a campfire), smoked venison and Mother Dunn's pies.
  - **Prices**: every item a trader would buy has a `price` (traders pay a twentieth).
  - **Plate** begins with the royal guard greaves; a full plate tier is for later (the knights' original grey armour art is kept for it).

  Dropped gear lies on the ground as itself (its icon), so you can see what it is before picking it up (the chunky flat styles still show a sack). To test without hunting, the ⚙ panel's **Loot** section has "Drop random loot" and "Drop ×5": random items pop out a few steps away.
- **Gear on the hero**: in the HD style the heroes are bare by default (skin, linen shorts, bare feet, their own hair and face; Masked is a pale spirit) and every item shows on all eight of them, in every animation (walk, idle, attack, jump, backflip): caps and helms fitted to each head, armour re-shading the chest and shorts, trousers the shorts and legs, boots, both shields, the amulet and each weapon (an empty weapon slot means bare hands). The New character screen shows each hero in the starting kit. The knights' original grey plate is kept in `tools/heroes/cast2.py` (`SIG_*`) as the art for a future plate tier. The flat styles (S1h, C2, C3, Silhouette) do not draw gear. Art sources: `tools/heroes/` (see its README).
- **Drops**: items pop out of whatever dropped them and are picked up by walking over them. Cows drop raw meat.
- **Keepsakes you can use** (`use` on an ItemDef, Aldric's whistle): click it in the bag to use it: it makes its sound and calls out (`Region.call`); whatever answers does (`EnemyDef.answers`), and if nothing does it says so. Then it needs a breather (`cd`, shown in its tooltip).
- **Food**: click raw meat in the inventory to eat it (+30 health). Each food fills you for 45 s (`MEAL` in `src/sim/Hero.ts`), and you can eat with 45 s of fullness or less left: two foods back to back, then you are full ("You're too full to eat"; the bag's tooltip says how long) until one is digested, so a bag of meat can't carry you through a fight. A bandage isn't food: one every 30 s (`BANDAGE_CD`).
- **Woodcutting**: click a tree (or press `B` next to one; `B` gathers whichever tree or rock is closest) to walk over and chop it. Four chops fell it: it drops 2–3 wood logs and leaves a stump that regrows after 40 seconds. Without an axe every chop takes twice as long.
- **Mining**: click a rock (or press `B` next to it) to walk over and mine it. Five swings break it into rubble: 1–2 stone, iron ore 35% of the time, a gold nugget 5% of the time. The rubble can be walked over and becomes a rock again after 75 seconds (never on top of someone). A **pickaxe** (tier 1, from slimes and goblins, or smithed at a forge) mines twice as fast; without one every swing takes three times as long. Tuning in `MINE` (`src/data/items.ts`).
- While you chop or mine, a bar over your head fills as the tree or rock gives way. Clicking it again doesn't hurry the swings: they come at the tool's pace, however often you click.
- **Rock collision**: a rock blocks a small circle around its boulder (`ROCK_R` in `src/sim/map.ts`), and against rocks a mover counts only its feet, so you stop where the sprites meet. Pathfinding still routes around standing rocks.
- **Crafting** (`K`, or the hammer on the menu bar; docked left so the inventory can stay open on the right). Recipes in `src/data/crafting.ts`, grouped by where they are made; each shows what it needs (have/need, red when short) and why it can't be made yet. A recipe with a `when` isn't shown (or made) until someone has taught it. Making takes time (`time` on each recipe: 2 s to cook, up to 6 s for a forge or forged gear): you stand still while a bar over your head fills, and the ingredients are used up only when it's done. Walking off, mounting or running short stops you (nothing is lost), and the recipe's button reads **Stop** while you work. Shift-click makes as many as you can, one after another.
  - **By hand**: a **campfire** (3 logs) or a **forge** (8 stone, 2 logs), built on a free tile next to you. A campfire burns for three minutes and lights up the night; a forge stays (and is solid).
  - **Campfire, cooking**: raw meat (+30 health) → cooked meat (+80 health).
  - **Forge, smelting**: 2 iron ore + 1 log → iron bar; 2 gold nuggets + 1 log → gold bar.
  - **Forge, smithing**: iron sword (3 bars, 1 log), woodcutter's axe and pickaxe (2 bars, 2 logs each), wooden shield (4 logs, 1 bar), iron kite shield (5 bars), iron helm (3 bars), amulet of vigour (2 gold bars). So mining is a second road to tier-2 iron gear.

## Creatures

Defined in `src/data/enemies.ts`, each with a behaviour:

- **Cow** (passive): grazes, never attacks, runs away when hit.
- **Slime** (neutral): ignores you until you hit it, then fights back; gives up if you walk away.
- **Skeleton** (hostile, night only): climbs out of the ground at dusk (out of the Greenmarch's barrows and Millbrook's churchyard), attacks on sight, crumbles to dust at dawn (no loot for crumbling).
- **Goblin, Goblin Shaman** (hostile): the first Greyfang raiders, camped in the east of the Greenmarch. **Ogre**: not placed yet.
- **Bone Hound** (hostile, night only): the old King's hunting dogs, fast, running the fields round Millbrook in packs. Drops fangs.
- **The Bell-Ringer** (boss, the bell tower): melee at first; from two thirds of his health he tolls the bell, sending a ring of sound across the floor (jump it) and calling the dead up through the floor; below a third the bell is cracked and he tolls twice as fast, rings in pairs, and stands out of breath after each toll.

- **Act II's wood** (`docs/act2.md`, *Enemies*): **wolves**, **thornback wolves** (packs that howl each other in), **boar**, **deer** (run; venison), **Weepwood spiders** (drop from the canopy, spit web), **spiderlings** and **egg sacs** (burn them before they hatch), **Mourning Lights** (lures: follow one home and the graves open), **mourners** (their wail heals the dead), **Smotherers** (go for fires), **hanged poachers** (archers from the Gallows Willow), the **Tower Guard** and **Tower crossbowmen** (shields up; slowing bolts; they call to each other), the **Sleepers** of the barrow, **goblin trappers** (snares and nets) and **wolf-riders** (fight on, on foot). Bosses: **the Brood Mother** (drops, sprays web, lays egg sacs, climbs back into the dark below half: `climbs`), **Bonespine** (howls up thornbacks, leaps at the furthest), the **outriders' chief**, the **search party's sergeant**, **the Thane under the Hill** (stamps up Sleepers; below half sings the fallen back up: interrupt it), and **Sir Garrick** (`Region.garrick`: two cuts and a lunge given away by a step back; shields up; from two thirds black roots hold one hero and he goes for them, and Aldric's whistle stops him dead and frees them; below a third his shield goes in the river, he fights faster and the Tower Guard march onto the bridge from both ends).

Each region places its own creatures (`spawns` in `src/data/regions`); art for the hound and the Bell-Ringer is in `tools/heroes/creatures.py`, and for Act II's in `tools/heroes/creatures2.py`.

**What a creature can do** (fields on its `EnemyDef`, carried out in `src/sim/Region.ts`; the wood's creatures in Act II are made of these):

- `casts`: spells with a cast bar, interrupted by Interrupt, Silence, Shield Bash or any stun: a **fireball** (the shaman's), a **heal** (a mourner's wail: the dead nearby get health back), a **raise** (a song: the dead who fell nearby lately climb up again, at most six at once), a **web** (a gob of web or a net at its foe, holding them; with `cone`, everyone in front of it).
- `missile`: shoots instead of striking: a bolt flies to where its foe stood and hits if they are still there (step aside and it misses), slowing them if it has `slow`.
- `shield`: now and then it raises its shield, and blows from in front of it do a quarter (a bleed gets round it; any stun drops it). A small shield shows on that side.
- `ambush`: waits unseen in the canopy (it can't be targeted or clicked) until a hero walks under it, then drops on them; a blow knocks it down early.
- `snares`: lays snares in the grass between itself and its foe (never underfoot); whoever steps in one is held (jump it).
- `rider`: when the mount falls, the rider fights on, on foot, as another kind.
- `hatch` (with the `inert` behaviour): an egg that wakes when a hero comes near and hatches some seconds later, unless it's burned first.
- `howl`: when it notices you, every creature that howls within reach comes running.
- `summon`: calls up help while it fights, from a boss phase on, never more than its cap at once.
- `leap`: crouches (you can see it), then leaps at the hero furthest off and lands on them; a stun in the crouch stops it.
- `lure`: never fights; as you come near it drifts toward its spawn's `to`, keeping just out of reach, and blinks on when struck.
- `smother`: goes for fires before people: walks to the nearest one burning and lies on it until it's out (kill it first). A fire being smothered dims as it goes.
- `dead`: one of the dead (a wail heals it, a song raises it); `answers`: stopped dead by a call from the bag (Aldric's whistle), and lets go of whoever it held.

**Held still** (a web, a net, a snare, roots: `Hero.hold`): no walking, jumping or dodging till it lets go (shown at their feet and across them); online the server refuses the browser's steps meanwhile, and the browser knows. **Slowed** (`Hero.slow`): walking slower for a while.

## Day / night

`src/sim/daylight.ts` keeps the clock (10 real minutes per day) and a gradient of the darkness colour to subtract at each hour. `src/phaser/lighting/` renders a quarter-resolution lightmap filled with that colour, stamps point lights into it as soft holes (castle torches with flicker, a small light on the player), and a post-effect shader composites `scene − lightmap²`. Adapted from the realms engine's lighting.

## Weather

`src/sim/weather.ts` (engine-free, ticked by `Game` like the day): the first 4–7 minutes are dry, then dry spells (10–30 min) and wet ones (rain about 2–4 min, a storm about 3–5) take turns. About two wet spells in five are storms, which build up after the rain has started and blow over before it stops. A game day being 10 minutes, it rains on a bit over half the days (always on the first) and storms on about one in five; about 15% of the time all told. During a storm, lightning comes about every 10 s: a candidate every 7 s, struck by chance, each near, out of sight, or far off. The schedule and the lightning come from a seed and the weather's own clock, so online the server sends `[clock, seed, rain, storm, wind, held]` with the day each second, and every browser in the room sees the same rain and the same strikes (PROTOCOL 4). The weather isn't saved. The log says when it starts to rain, when a storm rolls in and when it stops.

On screen (`src/phaser/render/WeatherFx.ts`): pixel rain streaks slanting with the wind, a crown splash where each lands (a widening ring on water), an overcast that darkens and cools the light (`Lighting.setWeather`: a light lifts the night's darkness in its pool but not the overcast's), darker clouds that drive faster in a storm, and lightning: a flash that lifts the darkness and washes the view pale, flickering twice when it's close, with a jagged pixel bolt and a jolt of the camera. The 3D views draw the same rain, splashes and bolts as one-pixel lines (`src/phaser/view3d/Rain3D.ts`). In point of view the sky turns grey, the fog closes in, and far strikes come down on the horizon. Indoors there's no rain, only the gloom, a dimmer flash and muffled sound.

Movement speed is constant in screen pixels for every direction (see `isoSpeedFactor` in `src/sim/map.ts`), so the 2:1 isometric projection doesn't make north-south travel feel slow.

### Skill icons

The action bar, spellbook and talent icons are hand-placed 20×20 pixel art in `tools/heroes/skillicons.py` (one object plus its effect, lit from the top-left, to the standard of the backpack icon). Digits in the grids are glow: drawn over the dark outline and never outlined, so sparks, wind, sound and auras read as light. Run `python3 skillicons.py out.png` to preview them magnified on the bar's parchment and the spellbook's dark frame, then `python3 gen_itemart.py` to write `src/phaser/render/skillIcons.ts`.

### Lore

The story bible is `docs/lore.md`: the world of Corvalis, the cast, the secret timeline, the acts and endings, bestiary, relics, Victoria's diary pages and notes for building quests. Read it in the browser at http://localhost:3000/lore.html while the server runs: `server.js` renders the Markdown (with `marked`) into the `docs/lore-viewer.html` template on every request, so the page holds the full text (browser reading mode works) and edits show up on reload. The raw file is at `/lore.md`.

The lore's illustrated plates (map, Thornhallow, the Blackthorn stages, portraits and more) are sepia-ink SVGs in `docs/lore-art/`, drawn by `tools/lore/plates.py` (`python3 tools/lore/plates.py [name ...]`) and placed in the Markdown as `<figure class="plate">`. A shareable copy is published as a claude.ai artifact: `npm run share:lore -- <out.html>` builds one self-contained page (`docs/lore-share.html` template, plates inlined), which is then republished to the same artifact.

## Classes

Three classes, chosen when you make a character (single player and multi player alike): **Warrior** (steel up close; Blade, Fury and Warden talents), **Archer** (arrows from afar, standing still to shoot; Marksman, Hunter and Ranger talents) and **Sorceress** (fire, frost and lightning from a staff, standing still to cast; Fire, Frost and Arcane talents). `src/data/classes.ts` holds what a class decides: its health, its auto-attack, its starting kit, its Rev presets and mobile wheels. Spells (`spells.ts`) and talent trees (`talents.ts`) each belong to a class; weapons and off hands too (`ItemDef.cls`), while armour, tools and amulets are for everyone. Personal loot is swapped to the killer's class (a sword drops as a longbow for an archer and a staff for a sorceress). The archer's design, every number, and how the classes were balanced against each other (a fight simulator running the real game) are in `docs/archer.md` (in the game: Settings › Documents › The archer); the sorceress's are in `docs/sorceress.md` (Settings › Documents › The sorceress).

## Online co-op

The plan, its decisions and where it stands are in `docs/online.md` (in the game: Settings › Documents › Online co-op plan).

- **Playing:** after loading, the main menu offers **Single Player | Multi Player**. Multi Player opens your characters on the server, on the same character screens as single player: make one, delete one, or pick one. Then the room list: open a room (Normal, Hard or Nightmare: the list shows each room's) or join one, with up to 8 players a room. A room is as hard as its host chose; a guest's own campaign keeps its own difficulty. Other computers on the network open `http://<this computer's address>:3000`. The badge at the top shows the room, who is here and the ping, and **Leave** goes back to the main menu. Online play never touches the single-player save.
- **The server is the game.** `server.js` serves the page and a WebSocket at `/ws`. Each room is a `Game` ticking 20 times a second, and each player gets a snapshot of their region with only the parts that changed (`src/server/Session.ts`). Messages are JSON, compressed on the wire.
- **The browser** (`src/net/NetSim.ts`) keeps a copy of the player's region built from the snapshots. It walks the player's own hero at once and tells the server where it went, and the server checks each step: speed, and nothing solid in the way. A refused step sends the hero back. Other heroes and creatures glide between snapshots. Everything else (casting, talking, trading, crafting, travel) goes to the server as a command from the whitelist in `src/net/commands.ts`. The dev settings' cheats work online while the server isn't in production mode.
- **Characters** (`src/server/Store.ts`) live on the server's disk in `server-data/` (gitignored; `QFV_DATA_DIR` moves it): accounts/, characters/ and deleted/, one JSON file each. A character is a single-player save plus a name and a look, so the same `Game.toSave` / `loadSave` code keeps them. They're saved every 5 seconds, on leaving, and when the server stops. Whoever opens a room brings their whole campaign (`Game.loadSave`); anyone joining brings only their hero (`Game.loadHero`) and keeps the story they see there (`mergeStory` in `src/sim/save.ts`). A browser's characters belong to the random key it keeps in localStorage (`qfv-account`, `src/net/account.ts`); there's no password yet. Rooms aren't kept: one closes 5 minutes after its last player leaves.
- **Quests in a room** (`Game.questStatus` / `acceptQuest` / `completeQuest`, `Hero.questLog`): the room keeps quest progress (shared kills and places); each hero keeps their own log (on it, or handed in). Accepting gives the quest to the whole party, and each player hands it in for their own reward, once per character. The first hand-in plays the quest's story effects. The log travels with the character (`Game.toSave` writes the hero's view; `loadHero` brings it into the next room).
- **Staying connected:** the server pings each browser at the WebSocket level every 10 seconds, which the browser answers even from a background tab, and lets a character go after 45 seconds of silence.
- **Duels** (`src/sim/Duel.ts`, `src/phaser/ui/DuelWindow.ts`), in the manner of WoW's: click another player and their card shows their class, level and duels won and lost, with **Challenge to a duel** (they must be within about eight tiles). They have 30 seconds to Accept or Decline, from a bar at the top of the screen. A yes plants a flag between you, a drum counts 3, 2, 1, and you fight with everything you have, each starting with the other as your target (red name, a red ring at their feet). Every spell works on a hero as on a creature: Whirlwind, Volley and Piercing Shot catch them, a bear trap snares them, a Mark or a Sunder is yours on them, a Concussive Shot slows them, and Charge, Mortal Strike and Shield Bash **stun** them (no walking, spells or swings; stars over their head). Their armor, a dodge and Sidestep count as against a creature's blow. The duel ends when one is down to their last hit point (nobody dies of a duel), **Yields**, or stays outside the ring round the flag for 10 seconds, or leaves the region or the room. Everyone there reads who won; both get back the health and mana they started with; wins and losses are kept with the character (`SaveData.duels`). A creature can still kill a duellist, which ends the duel with no winner. The rules of combat are written against creatures, so a duel opponent stands in as one: a `Rival` reads where they are and their health off the hero, passes stuns and slows on to them, and sends its damage through the duel (`Duels.strike` → `Hero.hurt`). Online the duel travels in the snapshot (`du`: mine; `dl`: the flags in my region; PROTOCOL 6) Once the fight is on, its bar steps aside to a small plate on the left under the frames, and **Yield** asks *Sure?* before it gives the duel away.
- **Trading between players** (`src/sim/Trade.ts`, `src/phaser/ui/PlayerTradeWindow.ts`): the player card's **Trade** asks them (within about eight tiles, not mid-duel); they have 30 seconds to say yes from a bar at the top. Then a table opens on the left for both: what you give (from your bag, click for one, shift-click for the stack, and gold), what they give, and **Accept trade**. Any change takes both accepts back; once both accept, everything changes hands at once, or nothing does if either no longer has it or a bag is too full. Only items with a price trade (keepsakes and quest rewards stay put). Walking more than about twelve tiles apart, a scene, a duel or leaving the region calls it off. Online the trade travels in the snapshot (`tv`; PROTOCOL 8).
- **Testing alone:** `npm run bot -- host "Bot room"` opens a room with a bot in it; `npm run bot -- join ABCD` sends one into room ABCD (`--cls archer` or `--cls sorceress` for an archer or a sorceress bot, `--difficulty hard` or `nightmare` for the room it opens); `npm run bot` lists the rooms. A bot takes up any duel it's challenged to and fights it, and any trade it's asked to (5 gold on the table, accepting once you do); `--challenge` has it challenge whoever comes near.

## Languages

English and Spanish. The game starts in Spanish on a computer whose first language the game speaks is Spanish, else in English; **Settings › Language** (and the buttons under the main menu) switches at once, and the choice is kept in this browser. The simulation, the server and the data stay in English: the browser translates whatever it shows (every Phaser text and every text in the HTML windows) by looking it up in `src/i18n/es/*.json`, keyed by the English, with `{0}` templates for text built around names and numbers, so text made by the server translates too. `src/i18n/README.md` explains how to write entries and holds the Spanish glossary; `npm run i18n` lists strings in the source with no translation yet, and in the browser `qfvI18n.missing()` lists what reached the screen untranslated. The design documents (the lore, the act specs) stay in English.
